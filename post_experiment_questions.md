# Post-Task Assessment: Question Catalogue

Full post-task assessment protocol a, comprising a semi-structured interview and a
participatory mapping exercise. 


## Administration notes

- **Format**: semi-structured interview, audio-recorded, followed by a participatory mapping exercise.

---

## 0. Introduction (~1 min)

> We want to know about your experience with the voice-picking system after
> the test with eye-tracking and the wearable device. We are not
> evaluating your performance, so you don't have to be worried. 

---

## 1. Spatial cognition, spatial practice and navigation (~5–7 min)

- Can you walk me through by drawing your routes of the task you just completed? *(participant draws route on shopfloor map)* (1. Draw where you went)
- Can you tell me do how you feel about certain areas using these stickers? Can you explain why? *(participant places stickers on the emotional map)* (2. Place a sticker for each area, coded by colour to feeling:)
- What landmarks or reference points do you follow to find your way inside the warehouse? and why? (e.g. numbers, aisle/zone labels, physical landmarks) *[navigation & wayfinding]*

*Participatory mapping using a printed floorplan/mental-map template and coloured stickers:*
  1. Draw where you went (using the floor plan of the respective warehouse)
  2. Place a sticker for each area, coded by colour to feeling:
    | Colour | Feeling |
    |---|---|
    | green | Relieved / assisted |
    | yellow | Calm / peaceful |
    | red | Confusion / disorientation |
    | blue | Frustration / stressful |
    | black | Boredom |


Method reference: Sustainable Calgary (participatoryplanning.ca).

----

## 2. Breakdown and Recovery

### 2.1 Situational breakdown taxonomy (~2–3 min)

> We noticed there are different types of interuption happen in the wareouse, namely:
  * System disruption — when you keep repeating a command and/or the system misrecognises your voice.
  * Loss of context — when you are interrupted by surroundings (someone talks to you, or asks for help) and are unsure what the system will say next.
  * Environmental factors — external obstructions (extreme temperature,noise) that make it harder to finish tasks or hear the system.
  * Decision-making** — complex cases (double ticket, system error, broken device, etc.) that leave you unsure what to do.

- Can you describe which one of the interuptions that to you? / OR /Can you describe a situation where you struggled durik task? is any from list above applicable to you?

### 2.2 Demand vs. recovery performance

- Demand: when doing task/experiencing the interuption, rate a scale of 1-10 of how much the demand of...
  * Mental demand: How much thinking, deciding, calculating, remembering, looking, searching did the task/problem gave you?
  * Physical demand: How much physical demand do you feel this problem/task?
  * Temporal demand: How much time pressure did you feel for this task/problem?

- Task recovery: when experiencing trying to recover, rate a scale of 1–10 on...
  * Effort: How hard did you have to work to resolve the problem?
  * Frustration: How stressed did you feel during the task?
  * Fairness: How fairly do you think voice-picking support was provided for your needs?

---

## 3 Recovery strategy and reflection
- What did you do to recover from the problem when you experience this interuption?
  - optional: What aspects were clear and understandable, and what seemed challenging or confusing? Why?
- In which way the system give more help to you that would help you best?
  - Automatically / 
  - Gradually / 
  - Only when I ask / 
  - Not at all (don't need help from the system)
  > Why?

---

## 4. Closing

> Thank you. Is there anything else you would change about the voice-picking system to make work easier?
